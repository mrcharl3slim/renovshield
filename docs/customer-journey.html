The journey

Three stages, three gates

Stage 1 · Day 0

Before signing

About 40 minutes, in one sitting
Enter quotesReview
  1. Opens the app with no sign-up

    Every call to action on the marketing page — including “Start Core Shield” and “Start Full-Arc Pro” — opens the same dialog offering John's example or blank. Picking one mints a private link and drops them straight in.

    “You'll get a private link to your project. Anyone with the link can view and edit it.”
  2. States the budget

    S$50,000 budget, S$10,000 deposit planned, 5% buffer for changes during works.

  3. Answers seven questions per firm

    HDB Directory of Renovation Contractors listing, CaseTrust accreditation, completion date in the contract, deposit asked, price escalation, cancellation fee.

  4. Types 33 prices

    Eleven line items across three firms. A blank cell is meaningful — it means that quote omits the item, and it is the single most important input on the screen.

  5. Reads the review

    A verdict, three firm cards, then price comparison, contractor checks, budget trade-offs and a copyable list of questions to ask. Fifteen concerns surfaced across the three quotes.

Firm B Headline S$39,000 Omits electrical works and final cleaning. Wants 50% up front, no completion date.
Firm B Like-for-like S$44,400 Once the two missing items are priced at the middle of the typical range.
Firm A Recommended S$48,400 Complete quote, 20% deposit, in the HDB directory, CaseTrust accredited.
Gate 1 — nothing has been paid yet

Compare the like-for-like total, not the headline price

Firm B looks S$9,400 cheaper than Firm A. Once the omissions are priced in, the real gap is S$4,000 — and it comes with a 50% deposit and no completion date. The app's recommendation is Firm A at S$50,820 including the buffer, with a note to find S$820 to stay on budget.

Stage 2 · Weeks 1–10

During works

Returns every week or two
MilestonesRecords
  1. Nominates the firm they signed with

    Contract value becomes that firm's quote plus any change orders logged in Records.

  2. Walks the site, ticks the checklist

    Each milestone carries its own checks — walls hacked to the agreed layout, debris removed, photos saved, no leaks after a water test.

  3. Pays only once the checklist is clear

    Paying writes a Payment record into the vault automatically and freezes the amount, so a later change order cannot rewrite what the record says was paid.

  4. Files everything as it happens

    Agreements, change orders, photo notes, payments and issues land on one searchable timeline. The S$240 for two extra power points in the study was agreed over WhatsApp on 20 November and logged the same day.

Gate 2 — enforced on the button itself

“Finish checks to pay”

The payment button is disabled until every check on that milestone is ticked, and says so. Money cannot run ahead of the work. The two tabs form a loop: paying writes a record, and change orders recorded raise the contract value the next payment is calculated from.

Stage 3 · Weeks 10–12

At handover

Days, under pressure to sign off
DefectsMilestones
  1. Walks the flat room by room

    Logs each defect by room and severity — a kitchen cabinet door that won't close flush, water pooling near the bathroom floor trap.

  2. Sends the list

    One button copies every un-checked defect as numbered plain text, ready to paste to the contractor.

  3. Moves each item along

    Open, then Fixed when the contractor says so, then Checked once the homeowner has seen it themselves. Only Checked counts.

  4. Releases the last 15%

    When the final defect clears, the banner flips from “Hold the final payment” to “Ready for handover” and the last milestone unlocks.

Gate 3 — the one with leverage

S$7,296 stays put while any defect is open

The Handover milestone cannot be marked paid while a single defect sits at Open or Fixed. In the worked example that is three defects, one of them major — so the banner reads “Hold the final payment”. This is the moment the whole product exists for: the homeowner still holds 15% when their leverage would otherwise be gone.

Honest read

Where the journey breaks

Ranked by how much damage each does to the journey above, not by how hard it is to fix.

  1. 01 The link is the only key Critical

    No account, no email, no recovery, no list of your projects. The URL is mentioned once in the opening dialog, then lives behind a small “Copy project link” button. A journey deliberately designed to span three months hangs entirely on the customer not losing a tab.

    Bites from day 0 onward
  2. 02 Thirty-three prices, typed by hand Critical

    There is no PDF upload and no OCR anywhere in the product. The promise is “compare three quotations”, and the wall sits squarely between the customer and the first moment of value — they must do all the work before seeing any of the payoff.

    Bites at stage 1, step 4
  3. 03 The pricing tiers do not exist High

    All five tabs are available to everyone. “Check a quote”, “Start Core Shield” and “Start Full-Arc Pro” are the same button wired to the same dialog. There is no payment step anywhere in the journey, so the journey you would demo and the one you would charge for are not yet the same.

    Bites across every stage
  4. 04 Typical prices are eleven fixed ranges Medium

    Identical for every project and inherited even by a blank one. “Checked against typical prices” currently rests on static seed figures rather than market data or anything specific to the customer's flat type.

    Bites at stage 1, step 5
  5. 05 Fixed at exactly three firms Medium

    The server accepts between one and six, but the interface offers no way to add or remove one. A homeowner holding two quotes, or four, has to work around the product.

    Bites at stage 1, step 3

The gates are self-reported. Nothing verifies that the work was done — the homeowner ticks their own boxes, and the money moves in their own bank. RenovShield is a discipline tool that makes withholding payment the default rather than an argument the customer has to start. It is not escrow, and the pitch should not imply that it is.
